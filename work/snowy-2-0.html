Snowy 2.0: Stakeholder Communication and Community Engagement

简介Summary

以澳大利亚 Snowy 2.0 抽水蓄能项目为案例，对照环评报告的承诺与之后的社区调查、公开发布，指出项目获批后社区沟通明显减弱，并提出两条建议：持续、独立地监测公众情绪；让社区意见在决策中看得见回应。

Using Australia's Snowy 2.0 pumped-hydro project as the case, I compared the engagement promises in the 2019 Environmental Impact Statement with later community research and public releases. The analysis shows engagement thinned sharply after approval, and makes two recommendations: continuous, independent sentiment monitoring, and closing the consultation loop so community input visibly shapes decisions.

1. 背景1. Context

作业要求从沟通与相关方管理的角度，对一个真实的大型项目做批判性分析：不复述案例，而是给出判断并用证据支持。

我选了 Snowy 2.0。它是澳大利亚在建最大的可再生能源项目，位于 Kosciuszko 国家公园内，由政府全资持有，建设周期超过十年。预算从 2019 年的 61 亿澳元涨到 2023 年的 120 亿，工期多次推迟。这样一个周期长、争议大、离社区近的项目，沟通做得好坏，直接影响它能不能持续拿到「社会许可」。

The brief was a critical analysis of a real megaproject through the lens of communication and stakeholder management: not retelling the case, but making a judgement and backing it with evidence.

I chose Snowy 2.0, Australia's largest renewable project under construction. It sits inside Kosciuszko National Park, is fully government-owned and will take more than a decade to build. Its budget rose from AUD 6.1 billion in 2019 to 12 billion in 2023, with repeated delays. For a long, contested project this close to communities, communication quality directly affects whether it keeps its social licence.

2. 我的思路2. How I approached it

先定评判标准。社区情绪随时在变，某一时刻的满意度说明不了沟通好不好。所以我没用「社区满不满意」当标准，而是问：这套沟通有没有服务于项目和社区的长期利益。

再拿公司自己的话当尺子。2019 年环评附录 I 里，公司自己写了三句承诺：沟通是双向的；社区反馈总体积极；会定期复盘。我沿时间线逐条核对它们后来有没有兑现：

  1. 2017–2019 年：认知调研、态度调查、社会影响评估，全部集中在审批前。
  2. 2019–2024 年：找不到任何公开的复盘记录。
  3. 2024 年：公司委托的独立机构 Struber 调研了 200 多人，44% 评价沟通差或很差，只有 21% 评价好；原住民代表反映，咨询时人选其实早已定好。
  4. 2025 年 10 月：一份主题是成本重估的新闻稿，通篇没有给出成本数字，标题强调的是进度按期。
  5. 2026 年：澳大利亚国家审计署认为项目 2023 年后的管理只是部分有效，缺少可靠的成本跟踪体系。

Set the yardstick first. Community sentiment moves constantly, so satisfaction at one moment proves little. Instead of asking whether the community was happy, I asked whether the communication served the long-term interests of both the project and the community.

Use the company's own words as the measure. Appendix I of the 2019 Environmental Impact Statement made three claims: engagement is two-way, feedback is overwhelmingly positive, and the approach will be reviewed regularly. I traced each claim along the timeline:

  1. 2017–2019: awareness research, an attitude survey and a social impact assessment, all before approval.
  2. 2019–2024: no public record of any review.
  3. 2024: research commissioned from Struber (200+ participants) found 44% rated engagement low or very low against 21% high; Aboriginal representatives found an advisory appointment had already been decided before they were consulted.
  4. October 2025: a media release about cost reassessment gave no cost figure and led with the project being on schedule.
  5. 2026: the Australian National Audit Office found post-2023 management only partly effective, with no reliable cost-tracking system.

3. 关键判断3. Key judgements

  1. 问题不在计划，在执行。2019 年的方案本身不差：相关方分得细，参与开始得早。真正的问题是精力全花在拿审批上，获批后就没人维护这段关系了。
  2. 不把两份调查硬比。2019 年问的是「支不支持项目」，2024 年问的是「沟通质量怎么样」，样本和方法都不同，所以我没有说「满意度从高跌到低」。我用 2024 年的数据证明的是另一件事：这个差评来自公司自己出钱、自己发布的调查，很难说成是外人的偏见。
  3. 认真回应反方观点。有人会说，信任下降主要是因为成本翻倍和工期延误，跟沟通关系不大。我承认成本和延误是重要背景，但「坏消息本身」和「怎么说坏消息」是两回事。审计署已经指出成本管理有问题，公司对外却仍在强调进度按期，这是沟通上的选择。
  1. The plan was fine; delivery was not. The 2019 plan segmented stakeholders well and started early. The failure was spending engagement effort to win approval and then leaving the relationship unattended.
  2. I did not force a comparison between the two surveys. 2019 asked about support for the project; 2024 asked about communication quality, with different samples and methods. I used the 2024 result for a different point: the criticism came from research the company itself commissioned and published, so it cannot be dismissed as outsiders' bias.
  3. I took the counter-argument seriously. One could say trust fell mainly because of cost and delay. Those matter, but bad news and how it is communicated are separate variables. While the auditor was flagging weak cost control, the company's public line still led with schedule. That was a communication choice.

4. 结论与建议4. Recommendations

  1. 把阶段性汇报换成持续的情绪监测。按固定周期独立调研、公开结果，挂靠项目现有的社区信息会，不另设机构，成本可控。我也写明了这个机制的局限：情绪有滞后，测到的常常是对几年前决定的反应，所以要看多期趋势和背后原因，不能只看单次读数。
  2. 让咨询形成闭环。每轮咨询后发一份简短的「你们提了什么，我们做了什么」记录：哪些意见改变了决定，哪些没有，为什么。如果决定已经定了，就直接说清楚，并说明是合同、法规还是工程限制导致的，而不是给个含糊答复。
  1. Replace stage-based reporting with continuous sentiment monitoring. Run independent research on a fixed cycle and publish it, attached to the project's existing community information meetings rather than a new body, to keep costs down. I also stated the limit: sentiment lags, often reacting to decisions made years earlier, so the mechanism should track multi-period trends and causes rather than single readings.
  2. Close the consultation loop. After each round, publish a short "you said, we did" record: which inputs changed the decision, which did not, and why. Where a decision is already fixed, say so plainly and give the reason (contract, regulation or engineering constraint) instead of a vague reply.

6. 和实际工作的关系6. Why it matters in practice

在建设单位实习时，我参与过招标和结算，见过项目方和施工方、审计方的沟通。这篇分析让我更清楚一件事：坏消息越晚说、说得越含糊，后面花的代价越大。做项目汇报时，我会主动给出数字和原因，而不是只报好消息。

During my owner-side internship I worked on tendering and final accounts and saw how the client, contractors and auditors communicate. This analysis sharpened one lesson: the later and vaguer bad news is delivered, the more it costs later. In project reporting I give the numbers and the reasons, not just the good news.

Executive Summary

The 2019 Environmental Impact Statement (Appendix I) described its approach as “two-way” and promised regular review. Yet the second survey did not come until 2024, five years later, and only after the project cost had doubled in 2023. Its result was that more residents rated engagement low than rated it high, 44% against 21%. The October 2025 cost-reassessment release, for its part, was titled around the project being on-schedule and gave no cost figure at all. Looked at as a whole, the project invested heavily in maintaining the relationship in its early stages, and then left that relationship unattended after approval. This paper judges the strategy against the long-term interests of the project and community, not community sentiment at any single moment, and proposes two recommendations: replacing stage-based reporting with continuous, independent, public sentiment monitoring; and closing the consultation loop so that community input has a visible influence on decisions.

1. Introduction

Snowy 2.0 is currently the largest renewable energy project under construction in Australia, located within Kosciuszko National Park, providing long-duration storage to the grid through pumped hydro. For a megaproject of this kind, built in an ecologically sensitive area, fully government-owned, and spanning more than a decade, whether it can sustain its social licence is not a secondary issue but one of the conditions determining whether it can proceed smoothly.

This paper does not evaluate the technical or economic success of the engineering itself. It focuses on a more specific question: to what extent did Snowy 2.0's communication genuinely support stakeholders' understanding, trust and participation?

My judgement is that the project's communication was procedurally competent but substantively ineffective: engagement effort was concentrated before approval, and grew thinner during delivery when it was most needed. The standard I use here is not community sentiment at any single point in time, which fluctuates in the short term and cannot on its own carry a judgement. The real standard is whether the communication served the long-term interests of both the project and the community.

Measured this way, the problem is not that the project failed to please the community at one moment, but that it handled communication in a way that worked in the short term and damaged the relationship in the long term: it spent its engagement effort to win approval, and overdrew the trust that should have lasted through the whole project.

2. A Well-Designed Engagement Plan

To judge whether Snowy 2.0's communication succeeded or failed, one has to admit its starting point was not low. The engagement framework in the 2019 Environmental Impact Statement was fairly complete: it addressed government, local community, industry and business groups, environmental groups, the Aboriginal community, and media as distinct stakeholders rather than “the public” in general; engagement began as early as the 2017 development stage, continuing the consultation of the earlier feasibility and exploratory works stages (EMM Consulting, 2019). Dare, Schirmer and Vanclay (2014) argue that social licence is accumulated through years of repeated contact and recedes once that contact stops. Otor, Bice and Henderson (2026) apply the same logic to major infrastructure and conclude that community acceptance has to be continually renewed; being granted once does not mean holding it for good. The 2019 plan was only an opening; the real question is whether, after approval, the company actually carried it out.

Appendix I states that the engagement approach would be reviewed regularly as the project progressed (EMM Consulting, 2019). But the 2017 awareness research and the 2018 attitude survey were both done before main construction began, and opinion gathered before construction says little about how people living beside the site felt in 2023. Without that review, the plan's other merits stopped being evidence of anything after 2019. So the next section tests one thing only: whether the review was done, what it found, whether that result was real, and whether it led to any improvement.

3. The Gap Between Promise and Fulfilment

Appendix I makes three claims: the approach is two-way (sharing, listening, acting); community feedback was, at the time, overwhelmingly positive; and the approach would be reviewed regularly (EMM Consulting, 2019). All three come from the same document. The company's own words therefore become the standard against which it is later judged.

The real problem is not the plan, but the distance between the plan and what was actually done. By 2024, the community insights research commissioned by Snowy Hydro and conducted by the independent agency Struber, drawing on more than 200 participants across four regions, found that 44% of respondents rated the company's community engagement as low or very low, against only 21% who rated it highly (Struber, 2024). More telling than the rating is one episode in the report: Aboriginal representatives, while being consulted on the choice of candidate for an advisory position, learned that the decision had in fact already been made, which led them to ask what the consultation was for, if there was no intention of adopting their input.

This is not two-way communication, but what Arnstein (1969) called tokenism: consultation keeps the form but loses the substance of influencing decisions. A strategy that called itself two-way stopped, in practice, at the lower rungs of the ladder.

It would nonetheless be hasty to conclude from this that the 2019 claim of overwhelmingly positive feedback was simply false. The two documents measured different things: 2019 asked whether the community supported the project, while 2024 asked about the quality of the company's communication; the two are not directly comparable in sample or method. Nor is the point of the 2024 figure that sentiment had turned negative, since sentiment on its own is not the standard I use here. What matters is the source: this unfavourable assessment came from research the company itself commissioned, funded and published, which makes it hard to dismiss as the view of hostile outsiders. What deserves questioning, then, is not the truth of any single statement, but how engagement effort was distributed over time: it was concentrated in the approval window (the 2017 awareness research, the 2018 attitude survey, the 2019 social impact assessment), while across the five years from 2019 to 2024 there is no public evidence the regular review commitment was ever met. The promise clearly existed; public evidence of its fulfilment does not.

The same pattern, dense before approval and silent during delivery, shows in the delivery-period communication. The October 2025 media release on the cost reassessment stated its subject clearly as cost, yet the whole text contained no real cost figure: it foregrounded the project being on-schedule and said only that up to nine months would be needed to complete the reassessment (Snowy Hydro, 2025). An announcement about cost withheld the one number it existed to provide. Di Maddaloni and Davis (2017) note that megaproject stakeholder management often serves only the party controlling key resources, excluding the local community from genuine communication planning and producing a means-ends decoupling. The evidence from Snowy 2.0 fits that description.

4. The Cost of Communication Choices

The effect of a communication strategy is finally borne by specific stakeholders. The most directly affected are the residents around and downstream of Kosciuszko National Park: they are within the range of construction impact, and they are exactly who the regular review commitment was meant for. Between the 2019 approval and the 2024 survey, was there any channel through which residents could tell the company how construction was affecting them? When Struber finally asked, 44% rated engagement low or very low. That figure in itself shows the channel had been closed for those five years.

One alternative explanation deserves serious attention: the fall in community trust may stem mainly from costs rising to three times the budget and repeated delays, not from communication. There is something to this; cost and delay are important background to the loss of trust. But the unfavourable facts and the way those facts are communicated are two different variables. The 2025 media release (Snowy Hydro, 2025) shows the company chose to emphasise schedule while delaying disclosure of the overrun. The 2026 Australian National Audit Office report found the project's post-2023 management only partly effective, noting the absence of a reliable cost-tracking system and an agreed completion baseline (Australian National Audit Office, 2026). That an independent auditor reached this view while public communication stayed on-schedule sharpens the point. Di Maddaloni and Davis (2017) find that a community shut out of real decision channels moves from suspicion to resistance, delay and cost. Ruijter et al. (2021) find that in large infrastructure projects trust accumulates slowly and drains fast. Cost and delay were the facts that had already happened; how the community would read those facts was decided by that October 2025 release.

5. Recommendations

Both of the following recommendations address the gap found in Section 3, and both target the same fault: a plan that treated 2019 approval as the end of the engagement work.

First, replace stage-based outcome reporting with continuous community-sentiment monitoring. The 2024 Struber survey proves the company can run this kind of research; the problem is that it ran it once, and only five years after promising regular review. Someone might say more frequent progress updates would fix this, but those are already what the company issues as construction reporting. What was missing between 2019 and 2024 was not information going out, but a channel to listen back. Mercer-Mapstone et al. (2017) argue that acceptance is moved by dialogue, the company not only informing but responding, an active process of listening and understanding, while one-way information does little. Sentiment measurement should therefore run on a fixed cycle; only adding updates would leave an already one-way ledger more unbalanced.

A deeper trade-off must also be faced. Sentiment is lagged: what is measured today may be the reaction, only now surfacing, to a decision made years ago, and surveying right after bad news can amplify a response that would otherwise settle. Yang et al. (2018) document the fixed-cycle Community Consultative Committee (CCC) as standard practice in Australian megaprojects. The mechanism should surface multi-cycle trends and the causes behind them, and can be attached to an existing body rather than a new one, which keeps the added cost low.

Second, close the loop of consultation. A short “you said, we did” record after each round already says a great deal: which inputs changed the decision, which did not, and why. Had such a record existed, the residents in the 2024 advisory-position episode would have known before the meeting how the choice was made. Holding more consultation sessions would not have solved it, because consultation was already taking place; what was missing was a visible link between what was said and what was decided (Arnstein, 1969).

This too has a cost. Someone who expected a say, on being told “this is already decided,” may feel worse than at a vague reply, because a vague reply at least leaves the possibility open. So the “already decided” message has to carry its reason, whether contract, regulation or engineering constraint removed the option. Even so, saying it plainly does what a vague answer cannot: it lets people know where to put their effort, and it stops consultation from claiming an influence it does not have.

6. Conclusion

The 2019 plan did its paperwork well: stakeholders were segmented, engagement started early, and it set a review standard for itself. But the 2019–2024 record shows the plan was left idle: review surfaced only once, in 2024, and only after the budget had doubled from AUD 6.1 billion to 12 billion (Australian National Audit Office, 2026); the 2025 cost announcement was written around schedule and did not point to any community research. Community attitude does fluctuate; but across the project's full life cycle, what deserves real attention is the purpose of engagement, rather than seeking approval for its own sake. On the day approval is granted, is the engagement truly finished? A plan built to sustain a relationship has to run to the last section of tunnel.
