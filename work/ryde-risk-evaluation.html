Ryde Hospital Redevelopment is a AUD 526.8m NSW Government project built while the hospital stays open. I wrote the risk evaluation: risk criteria and two override rules under ISO 31000, evaluation of five key risks, six tie-break criteria to rank equal scores, an executive risk overview, and the conclusion that the project may proceed only on conditions.

1. 背景1. Context

作业要求小组为一个真实的公共项目编写风险管理计划，读者是项目高层。我们选了 Ryde 医院改扩建：新建急症服务楼、扩充临床服务，主体预计 2027 年底完工，余下工程延续到 2028 年。最大的难点在于医院不能停：施工期间急诊、手术、住院都要照常运转。

全组六人分章节完成：项目背景和目标、风险识别、风险分析、风险评价、风险应对。我负责第 4 章风险评价，承上接住风险分析的打分结果，启下告诉管理层哪些风险必须先处理、项目能不能推进。

The group wrote a risk management plan for a real public project, addressed to senior executives. We chose Ryde Hospital Redevelopment: a new Acute Services Building and expanded clinical services, with the main building due in late 2027 and remaining works into 2028. The hard constraint is that the hospital cannot close: emergency, surgery and inpatient care must keep running throughout.

Six members each wrote chapters: context and objectives, identification, analysis, evaluation and treatment. I wrote Chapter 4, which takes the analysis scores and tells executives which risks must be handled first and whether the project can proceed.

2. 我的思路2. How I approached it

先分清「分析」和「评价」。风险分析回答「多可能、多严重」；风险评价回答「所以要做什么」。ISO 31000 第 6.4.4 条的做法是把分析结果和事先定好的准则比较，所以第一步是把准则定清楚。

  1. 四级准则。按可能性乘后果的分数分为低（1–4）、中（5–9）、高（10–16）、极高（17–25），每一级写明结论（要不要处理）和汇报对象（谁负责、多久向谁报告）。
  2. 两条兜底规则。单纯相乘有个漏洞：「很少发生但后果极重」和「经常发生但后果轻微」可能同分。所以我加了两条：后果为 5 的风险至少定为高；影响医院安全与运营连续性（目标 O3）且后果大于等于 4 的，也至少定为高。
  3. 逐项评估。5 项风险对照准则：R02（主体工程招标价上涨）和 R04（施工期间医疗业务连续性）为高，R01（审批延误）、R03（运营经费不足）、R05（启用时人手不足）为中，没有触发兜底规则。
  4. 排优先级。分数只是起点。我列了 6 条补充标准，按重要性排序：影响几个目标、是否涉及患者安全；后果能否挽回；与其他风险的连锁关系；发生速度和预警期；可控程度；公众关注度。

Separate analysis from evaluation. Analysis asks how likely and how severe; evaluation asks what to do about it. ISO 31000 Clause 6.4.4 compares analysis results with predefined criteria, so the first job was to set those criteria clearly.

  1. Four levels. Likelihood × consequence gives Low (1–4), Medium (5–9), High (10–16) and Extreme (17–25), each with a required action and a reporting line.
  2. Two override rules. Multiplying scores hides the difference between "rare but severe" and "frequent but minor". So any risk with consequence 5 is at least High, and any risk to hospital safety and continuity (objective O3) with consequence 4 or more is at least High.
  3. Evaluate each risk. R02 (tender price increase) and R04 (business continuity during construction) are High; R01 (approval delay), R03 (recurrent operating funds) and R05 (workforce at commissioning) are Medium. No override is triggered.
  4. Prioritise. Scores are only a starting point. Six criteria, in order: objectives affected and patient safety; reversibility; links to other risks; speed of onset; controllability; public visibility.

3. 关键判断3. Key judgements

  1. R04 和 R02 同为 16 分，R04 排第一。R04 影响三个目标（O3、O4、O6），牵涉患者安全；R02 只影响成本（O6）。决定性的区别是能不能挽回：施工中断医疗或出安全事故，几乎没有补救余地；而成本上涨可以靠价值工程、调整范围和不可预见费部分消化。R02 确实更难控制（由市场价格决定），但「难控制」限制的是应对手段，不会让后果更严重。
  2. R01 和 R05 同为 9 分，R01 排在前面。R01 审批延误处在因果链上游：审批一拖，工期就长，市场价格波动的时间更长（推高 R02），医院边运营边施工的时间也更长（推高 R04）。R05 要到启用阶段才出现，有充足时间招聘和培训，也不会引发其他风险。所以 R01 虽然只是「中」，也要进管理层视线。
  3. R03 分数最低，排在最后，但不删。运营经费由地方卫生区和州卫生部负责，不在建设团队手里，后果要到移交后才显现。所以保留在登记册里持续监测，但建设期不占用管理层注意力。
  4. 指出风险矩阵本身的局限。矩阵上 R02 和 R04 落在同一格，R01 和 R05 也在同一格，矩阵分不出它们，因为它只看单个风险，不看风险之间的连锁。所以最终排序以补充标准为准，而不是以格子位置为准。
  5. 给出有条件的结论。没有风险到达「极高」，项目可以推进。所以管理层要决定的不是「做不做」，而是「批不批应对方案和资金」：R02、R04 必须先有落实了资金的应对方案，相关工作才能开展。我也写明了前提：如果方案不批或钱不到位，这个评价结论就不再成立。
  1. R04 ranks above R02, though both score 16. R04 affects three objectives (O3, O4, O6) and patient safety; R02 affects only cost (O6). The decisive difference is reversibility: a clinical disruption or safety incident leaves little room for remedy, while cost increases can be partly absorbed through value engineering, scope changes and contingency. R02 is less controllable, but that limits the responses available rather than increasing the severity.
  2. R01 ranks above R05, though both score 9. Approval delay sits upstream: it lengthens the schedule, which prolongs exposure to market prices (R02) and extends live-hospital construction (R04). R05 appears only at commissioning, leaves long lead time and drives no other risk.
  3. R03 ranks last but stays on the register. Operating funds are owned by the local health district and NSW Health, not the capital team, and consequences appear after handover. It is monitored, not escalated.
  4. I named the limit of the matrix. R02 and R04 share a cell, as do R01 and R05. The grid cannot separate them because it ignores links between risks, so priority comes from the criteria, not the grid position.
  5. A conditional conclusion. No risk is Extreme, so the project may proceed. The executive decision is not whether to proceed but whether to fund and authorise treatment: R02 and R04 need funded plans before the related work starts. If plans are not approved or funded, the conclusion no longer holds.

5. 老师反馈与不足5. Feedback and what I would improve

全组得分 14/20。老师主要批评的是整份计划只评估了 5 个风险，剩余风险（实施应对措施后的风险）没有评估。

  1. 风险数量少是全组在风险识别阶段的取舍，但评价章节也受影响：只有 5 项，排序的说服力有限。
  2. 剩余风险其实和我的章节直接相关。我写了「如果应对方案不落实，结论不成立」，但没有往下一步走：应对之后每项风险降到几分、还要不要管理层盯着。如果重写，我会在第 4 章末尾加一张「处理前、处理后」的对比表，让评价形成闭环。

The group scored 14/20. The marker's main criticism was that the plan evaluated only five risks and did not evaluate residual risk after treatment.

  1. Only five risks was a group decision at identification stage, but it limits how convincing the ranking can be.
  2. Residual risk does bear on my chapter. I stated that the conclusion fails if treatment is not funded, but stopped short of the next step: what each risk scores after treatment and whether it still needs executive attention. In a rewrite I would add a before-and-after table at the end of Chapter 4 to close the loop.

小组展示：向高管讲 90 秒Group presentation: 90 seconds for executives

报告交完后，小组把风险管理计划做成给高管的简报，录视频提交。我讲第 5 页风险评价，约 1 分 30 秒。讲稿只说三件事：10 分以上就必须处理；R04 和 R02 同分，但医疗中断「没有撤销键」，所以 R04 排第一；R01 只是中等，却是第一块多米诺骨牌。

全组得分 6.5/10。老师肯定我们把风险讲成「原因 → 事件 → 后果」，并给出了「相关工作开始前先给 R04、R02 拨款」的高管决策。指出的问题有几处和我这页直接相关，我都认同：

  1. 矩阵没有在幻灯片上定义可能性和后果的等级，听众只看到分数，不知道 4 分代表什么。
  2. R03 出现了两次，R05 漏掉了，说明合稿时没有逐页核对风险编号。
  3. 升级路径只写到项目总监和发起人，应该写到审计与风险委员会（新州财政部指引 TPG24-08）。

这些让我意识到：给高管的简报，每个数字都要能在同一页上读懂，每条路径都要走到最终负责的机构。

After the report, the group turned the plan into a recorded executive briefing. I presented slide 5, risk evaluation, in about 90 seconds, making three points: anything scoring 10 or more must be treated; R04 and R02 tie, but a clinical disruption has no undo button, so R04 comes first; R01 is only Medium but is the first domino.

The group scored 6.5/10. The marker praised framing risks as cause → event → consequence and the executive decision to fund R04 and R02 treatment before related work starts. Several criticisms touch my slide, and I accept them:

  1. The matrix did not define likelihood and consequence levels on the slide, so the audience saw scores without knowing what a 4 meant.
  2. R03 appeared twice and R05 was missing, so risk IDs were not checked slide by slide at merge.
  3. Escalation stopped at the Project Director and Sponsor; it should reach the Audit and Risk Committee (NSW Treasury TPG24-08).

The lesson: in an executive briefing every number must be readable on the same slide, and every escalation path must reach the body that is ultimately accountable.

6. 和实际工作的关系6. Why it matters in practice

风险登记册在很多项目里只是一张打分表。这一章让我练习的是打分之后的判断：分数一样时先管哪个、为什么，以及怎样把结论写成管理层可以直接批准或否决的事项。

On many projects a risk register is just a scoring sheet. This chapter trained the judgement after scoring: which risk to handle first when scores tie, why, and how to frame the result as something executives can approve or reject.

4. Risk Evaluation

4.1 Purpose and Basis of Evaluation

While risk analysis focuses on the likelihood of a risk occurring and the severity of its consequences, risk evaluation addresses a different question: what action should be taken regarding the risk based on the criteria adopted for this evaluation? In accordance with ISO 31000 Clause 6.4.4, the fundamental approach to risk evaluation involves comparing the results of the risk analysis against established risk criteria to determine whether further action is required. Depending on the comparison, the evaluation may conclude that existing controls should be maintained, or that project objectives need to be reconsidered.

4.2 Risk Criteria

This section sets out the level of risk the project is prepared to accept, and the action required at each level. Consistent with ISO 31000 Clause 6.3.4, the criteria set out below are aligned with the project objectives (Section 1.2), the likelihood definitions (Section 3.2) and the consequence scales (Section 3.3).

Table 10. Risk Criteria

5–9MediumTolerable to a degree but requires proactive management; maintain and monitor existing controls; record treatment measures in the register with effort commensurate with cost-effectiveness.Designate a risk owner to report regularly to the Project Director.

Override Rules

The following two rules apply regardless of the calculated score:

  1. Any risk with a consequence score of 5 must be rated at least High;
  2. Any risk impacting objective O3 (hospital safety and operational continuity) with a consequence score of 4 or higher must be rated at least High.

No risks currently in the register trigger these rules. These override rules are in place to address situations where:

  1. Ratings may change during delivery;
  2. Product-based scoring cannot distinguish between "rare but severe" and "frequent but minor" scenarios, which require different management approaches. Override rules ensure that severity is not diluted by low probability.

Assumption: The rating thresholds primarily reflect the team’s judgment regarding the level of risk the project should bear. These thresholds ensure that any risk rated High or above requires a treatment plan with secured funding before the associated work proceeds.

4.4 Prioritization

Risk scores alone do not provide a sufficient basis for ranking. R02 and R04 both score 16, while R01 and R05 both score 9. To resolve these ties, the team applied six additional criteria, listed below in descending order of importance.

Prioritization Criteria

  1. Number and criticality of affected objectives: Specifically, whether multiple objectives are affected and whether the impact directly concerns patient safety and clinical services.
  2. Reversibility of consequences: Consequences that can be remedied through existing mechanisms are ranked lower than those that cannot.
  3. Interconnectedness with other risks: Whether the risk triggers other risks or stems from them.
  4. Speed of onset and warning period: Risks that manifest rapidly with short warning periods allow for less response time.
  5. Controllability: Whether direct action can be taken to address the risk.
  6. Visibility to stakeholders: For instance, incidents involving public healthcare facilities are subject to immediate public scrutiny and accountability.

Differentiating Risks with Equal Scores

R04 vs. R02 (Both scored 16)

R04 and R02 are both scored 16, but R04 ranks higher. R04 affects three objectives—O3, O4 and O6—and therefore engages hospital operational continuity and patient safety; R02 affects only O6 (cost). The decisive difference lies in reversibility. A clinical service disruption or patient safety incident during construction would leave little room for remediation, whereas R02’s financial consequences can be partly absorbed through value engineering, scope reprioritisation and construction contingency. It is true that R02 is less controllable, since it is driven by exogenous market prices, but low controllability limits the range of feasible responses rather than increasing the severity of the outcome. Because a mechanism for containing financial exposure is already in place, priority should go to the risk that is irreversible and affects multiple objectives.

R03

R03 has the lowest score at just 6 points; however, the approach to managing it is constrained by factors beyond the score itself. Responsibility for recurrent operational funding rests with NSLHD and NSW Health, not the capital project delivery team; the consequences of any shortfall emerge in the operational phase following handover. Consequently, R03 remains on the register with monitoring-focused treatment (see Section 5), but ranks last for executive attention during delivery.

R01 and R05 (Both Rated 9)

R01 and R05 also have the same score, but R01 ranks higher. The reason lies in its position upstream in the causal chain: approval delay extends the project schedule, which in turn prolongs exposure to market movement (R02) and lengthens the live-construction window (R04). R01’s management value therefore exceeds what its standalone rating suggests. R05, by contrast, does not manifest until commissioning; it provides a long lead time for recruitment and training and does not drive other risks.

The following figure plots the five risks on the Section 3.4 matrix, shaded by the criteria bands in Section 4.2, with the priority order from Section 4.4 marked alongside.

Two features of the matrix are significant. R02 and R04 occupy the same cell, while R01 occupies the same coordinates as R05. The grid therefore cannot distinguish between risks that this evaluation ranks differently: it records each risk in isolation, without capturing its effect on the likelihood of other risks. Priority is therefore determined by the criteria in Section 4.4 rather than by grid position. Overall, exposure is concentrated in two cells rather than dispersed across the matrix, and the highest-rated risks are probable and material rather than remote and catastrophic.

4.6 Implications for Project Progression

The project may proceed, but not without conditions. No risk reaches the Extreme level, which is the only level at which proceeding would itself be called into question. This is consistent with Infrastructure NSW’s assessment of the Final Business Case.

The executive-level decision, therefore, is not whether to proceed but whether to fund and authorise treatment for the priority risks. R02 and R04 require funded treatment plans before the relevant work can proceed. R01, although rated Medium, warrants executive attention because it is the common upstream cause of both. Further details are provided in Section 5.

It should be noted that if the aforementioned treatment plans are not approved, or if they are approved but funding is not secured, the conclusions of this evaluation will no longer hold. In such a scenario, R02 and R04 would remain at a High level in the absence of funded treatment, and the requirements associated with this risk level cannot be satisfied merely through ongoing monitoring.
